-------

This paragraph is here on purpose, as something long enough to actually watch
change. Cycle the font and the letterforms change under you; cycle the width
and the measure gets narrower or wider. The colours come from the scheme you
picked (Haki if you never did), and dark/light flips the whole palette without
touching the scheme itself.

-------

* The cycles

Two settings are cycles: each press advances one step and wraps around at
the end. The step you land on is remembered.

** Font mode

Five modes, and *Mixed is the default* for a first visit.

| Mode            | Prose                | Structure, meta, UI | Code |
|-----------------+----------------------+---------------------+------|
| Mixed           | serif                | sans                | mono |
| Serif           | serif                | serif               | mono |
| Sans            | sans                 | sans                | mono |
| Mono            | mono                 | mono                | mono |
| Browser default | your browser's font  | your browser's font | mono |

Two things never move. *Code, =verbatim=, and preformatted blocks are always
monospace*, in every mode. And *headings always use the display face*, outside
the cycle entirely. /Browser default/ is for readers who have set a font they
like in their own browser: the site steps aside and uses it.

** Content width

Steps: *Normal* (default, about 830px), then *Wide*, *Full* and *Narrow*.

Normal is a comfortable measure for prose. Wide and Full help with wide code
blocks and tables; Narrow suits a large screen if you like short lines. Browser
zoom still works on top of any of them.

* Dark / light

This flips the *variant*, not the scheme. Until you press it, the site follows
your system setting; once you press it, your choice wins and is remembered.

If the scheme you are on has both a dark and a light palette, it swaps. If it has
only one, nothing pretends to work: the page tells you there is no such variant
and opens the scheme list, so you can pick one that has it.

* Colour schemes

** Where they come from

Three different sources, deliberately:

- *Haki* is mine, hand-written as CSS, and it is the default.
- *Modus* follows Protesilaos Stavrou's [[https://protesilaos.com/emacs/modus-themes][Modus themes]] for Emacs, with both a
  dark (Vivendi) and a light (Operandi) variant.
- *Everything else* comes straight from [[https://github.com/tinted-theming/schemes][tinted-theming/schemes]], the base16
  community collection, unmodified.

** Only readable ones

Every scheme is checked when the site is built. Body text needs a contrast of at
least 7:1 against the background, links 4.5:1, and headings 3:1. Schemes that fail
are left out rather than patched, so *140 of the 306* make it into the list.

The list opens with my curated families, marked /light and dark/: Haki, Modus,
Gruvbox and Tokyo Night have both variants, the rest are dark only. All the
other readable schemes follow, by name.

** How the whole thing works

Every scheme is just sixteen colours, =base00= through =base0F=, and the entire
UI is built from a handful of semantic names that point at those sixteen. So
=--link= means "=base0D=", and swapping a scheme means swapping sixteen values
while every rule in the stylesheet keeps working untouched.

That is the reason hundreds of imported editor themes can drive a website they
were never designed for. It also means schemes cost almost nothing: the curated
ones are plain CSS, and the rest are fetched only when you open the list.

Open the list with the scheme icon in the header, or open the command palette
and type =t= followed by a space. Moving through the list with the arrow keys
previews each scheme on the real page, and closing the list puts it back.

* The command palette

One dialog does search and every command. Open it with the *M-x* button in the
header, or with =x=, =Ctrl-K=, =Ctrl-S=, or =Alt-x= (Emacs =M-x=), whichever your
fingers already know.

| Type this      | Get                                          |
|----------------+----------------------------------------------|
| anything       | pages, tags, commands, and full-text results |
| =t= then space | every readable colour scheme, by name        |

=Esc= closes it. Backspace at an empty prefix goes back to the default list.

*Palette shortcut on / off* turns the =x= key off if you need your browser's
own =x= back, or use voice input where a stray letter fires a command. The
other open-palette keys keep working either way. This is
[[https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts.html][WCAG 2.1.4 Character Key Shortcuts]].

* Lists

Every list on the site (the Log, tags, Wander, Media, Quotes and more) has a
filter box on top. Type to narrow it down, =#emacs= to match a tag, =/= to jump
into the box and =Esc= to clear it. Next to it, *List* and *Grid* change how the
list is laid out, and the site remembers your pick.

* Reading a post

- *Headings* get a =#= link on hover, so any section is directly linkable.
- *References* are collected automatically from the links in the post and
  numbered at the end. *See Also* fills the leftover space with related posts,
  and only when there is room.
- *Comments* have no backend at all. The box composes a pre-filled email and
  hands it to your mail client. Embrace email!
- *Pose* entries are folded under their date and title; the newest one starts
  open, and a link to an entry opens it.
- *The site title* has a small secret if you hover over it, or click it. Easter egg shows random quote in popup toast.

* What gets stored

A few keys in =localStorage=, all starting with =emacs-=, and no cookies.

| Key                    | Holds                                         |
|------------------------+-----------------------------------------------|
| =emacs-theme=          | dark or light, once you have pressed it       |
| =emacs-scheme-fixed=   | the curated scheme you picked                 |
| =emacs-custom-palette= | any other scheme you picked from the list     |
| =emacs-font-mode=      | the font mode                                 |
| =emacs-width=          | the content width                             |
| =emacs-list-view=      | list or grid                                  |
| =emacs-keys-off=       | palette shortcut disabled                     |
| =emacs-wander-colors=  | your scheme's colours, for the Wander console |

Clearing site data resets everything to defaults. Nothing here identifies you,
and none of it leaves your browser. The site does load one analytics script,
[[https://umami.is/][Umami]], which counts visits without cookies or personal data.

* How my site is put/designed together

Written down mostly because people ask.

- Content is *Org-mode*, written in Emacs and built with [[file:log/denden.org][Denden]], my own small
  static site generator on top of Org's =ox-publish=.
- One stylesheet, in [[https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@layer][cascade layers]], styling plain HTML elements first and a
  short list of shared pieces for the rest.
- One small deferred script. The site reads fine without it; it only adds the
  controls above.
- Your scheme, font and width are applied *before the first paint*, so there is
  no flash of the wrong colours on load.
- =prefers-reduced-motion= is respected: nothing spins or sways unless motion is
  welcome, and then only on hover or focus.
- Every interactive thing is reachable by keyboard and has a visible focus ring.

More detail on the stack is on the [[file:colophon.org][Colophon]] page, and the whole source is
[[https://github.com/idlip/hakimacs][on GitHub]] if you would rather just read it.
