* OBTF's philosophy pitch

Their whole argument boils down to a handful of lines:

#+begin_quote
Your entire life, notes, tasks, ideas, logs, in one text file.
Just =.txt=.
#+end_quote

#+begin_quote
The best productivity system is the one you actually use. And nothing is simpler than a text file.
#+end_quote

#+begin_quote
Search beats categories
#+end_quote

No vendor lock-in, speed, offline by default, permanence. All fair, and none of it is specific to a bare =.txt= file. It's specific to *plain text*. You make use of many tools to suite your needs instead of you learning and adapting to a system.

* Where Org gives you more

"Search beats categories" is true until the file gets big enough that search alone stops being enough, and at that point you want *structure you can turn on when you need it*, not structure forced on you from day one.

I still keep a =datetree=, Year > Month > Day > entry, which gives me OBTF's own "newest entries first, just append and go" model:

#+begin_src org
  ,* 2026
  ,** 2026-10 October
  ,*** 2026-10-07 Tuesday
  ,**** <whatever I'm capturing>            :tag1:tag2:
#+end_src

The difference from a flat file is that I'm not stuck with chronology as the *only* axis. A few things I still reach for on top of the datetree:

- =org-sparse-tree= (=C-c /=): narrow the whole tree down to just the headings matching a tag or
  property, without touching the file itself.

- =consult-org-heading= / =consult-line= (from the =consult= package): fuzzy-jump straight to a
  heading or line, which is the actual "search beats categories" experience, just inside a
  structured file instead of a flat one.

- =org-ql=: for queries that are more than a single tag match, closer to a real query language over
  my notes than regexp-over-a-text-file ever was.

- =#+select_tags=: export just the headings carrying a given tag into a throwaway buffer, which
  behaves like a temporary, generated "file" scoped to one topic, without ever having to actually
  split the real file.

- [[https://github.com/toshism/org-super-links][org-super-links]]: Package to backlink org headings to one another.

- =org-agenda=: Best way to get organized and plan for time. Paired with note taking, the agenda interface is so powerful that even I've underused it mostly to just get stuff done.

- Tags: More detail on the tagging convention I use (the =l@=/=n@=/=p@= prefixes) is in [[file:obtf-org-mode.org][the older post]].

* Conclude
These might sound very much tied to emacs, yes thats mostly true. Environment so perfect that it can be called a monolith of emacs. But alas, people have ported org mode inspired modes and packages to various other ecosystem.

- [[https://github.com/nvim-orgmode/orgmode][orgmode.nvim]]: OG clone for neovim
- [[https://github.com/xheisenbugx/org.nvim][org.nvim]]: New and AI assisted clone of complete org library
- [[https://github.com/nvim-neorg/neorg][neorg]]: Future of organizing life in neovim
- [[https://github.com/rrajath/grove][grove]]: Beautiful org mode first task/notes android app
- [[https://github.com/orgzly-revived/orgzly-android-revived][orgzly-revived]]: Org in mobile at ease
- [[https://github.com/amake/orgro][orgro]]: Good org mode reader for android+IOS

PS: I use official [[https://www.emacswiki.org/emacs/EmacsOnAndroid][Emacs on Android]] as well bringing the same environment on the phone (~_~;)

Ultimately only thing that is required with note taking or system is to always review, revise, repeat and process them.
